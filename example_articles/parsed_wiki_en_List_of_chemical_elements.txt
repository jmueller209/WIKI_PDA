118 [chemical elements][1] have been [identified][2] and named officially by [IUPAC][3]. A chemical
element, often simply called an element, is a type of [atom][4] which has a specific number of
[protons][5] in its [atomic nucleus][6] (i.e., a specific [atomic number][7], or Z).

The definitive visualisation of all 118 elements is the [periodic table of the elements][8], whose
[history][9] along the principles of the [periodic law][10] was one of the founding developments of
modern [chemistry][11]. It is a tabular [arrangement][12] of the elements by their chemical
properties that usually uses abbreviated [chemical symbols][13] in place of full element names, but
the linear list format presented here is also useful. Like the periodic table, the list below
organizes the elements by the number of protons in their atoms; it can also be organized by other
properties, such as [atomic weight][14], [density][15], and [electronegativity][16]. For more
detailed information about the origins of element names, see [List of chemical element name
etymologies][17].
